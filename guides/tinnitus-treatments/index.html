Key message

The short answer

Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) has the strongest evidence of any tinnitus treatment — for reducing distress and improving quality of life, not for making the sound quieter. No treatment has been established as a universal cure for tinnitus. Beyond CBT, the best-supported options depend on your situation — hearing loss, sleep problems, and tinnitus subtype all change the picture.

Two different questions

Loudness

Did the sound itself get quieter?

Distress

Did it bother people less, even if the sound stayed?

The current top-ranked treatments

Rankings depend on how evidence dimensions are weighted. In our stress test of six reasonable weighting schemes, only two positions were fully stable (digital CBT programs and CBT for insomnia); CBT ranked #1 in five of six schemes. Positions marked 'weighting-sensitive' can move several places under defensible alternative weightings — treat the order as a guide, not a verdict.

How to read "strongest evidence"

  • We rate loudness and distress separately (most good evidence is for distress)
  • Require independent replication before calling anything established
  • Show who funded the research
  • And label ranking positions that are sensitive to how you weigh the evidence
