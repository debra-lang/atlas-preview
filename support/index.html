Support and crisis lines

Living with tinnitus can be hard, and you don't have to manage it alone. This page lists crisis lines for anyone who needs to talk to someone now, and organizations that support people with tinnitus.

If you need help now

If you are thinking about suicide, or feel you cannot keep yourself safe, please talk to someone now. These services are free and confidential, and staffed by trained people. If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number.

United States

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: Call or text 988, or chat online (24/7). Website
  • Veterans Crisis Line: Dial 988, then press 1, or text 838255 (24/7). Website

United Kingdom

  • Samaritans: Call 116 123 (24/7). Website
  • Shout: Text SHOUT to 85258 (24/7). Website

Ireland

  • Samaritans Ireland: Call 116 123 (24/7). Website
  • Text 50808: Text HELLO to 50808 (24/7). Website Verified on the HSE (Irish health service) page; the service's own site was unavailable when checked

Canada

  • 9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline: Call or text 9-8-8 (24/7). Website

Australia

  • Lifeline: Call 13 11 14, text 0477 13 11 14, or chat online (24/7). Website

New Zealand

  • 1737, Need to talk?: Call or text 1737 (24/7). Website

Germany

  • TelefonSeelsorge: Call 0800 111 0 111 or 0800 111 0 222, or 116 123 (24/7). Website

Anywhere else

Find A Helpline: Free helplines in more than 175 countries. Run by ThroughLine; helplines verify their details directly.

Tinnitus Evidence is an information website. It cannot provide counselling, does not monitor messages, and cannot follow up with anyone.

Tinnitus support organizations

These charities and associations offer information, helplines and support groups for people with tinnitus.

  • Tinnitus UK (United Kingdom): Free helpline 0800 018 0527 (Monday to Friday, 10am to 4pm UK time), webchat, text and email (details on their website), support groups and a free online course.
  • American Tinnitus Association (United States): Information line 800-634-8978, support-group directory and peer volunteers.
  • Chime: tinnitus support (Ireland): Information and one-to-one support for people with tinnitus.
  • Deutsche Tinnitus-Liga (Germany): Peer advice line and self-help groups (in German).
  • Acouphènes Québec (Canada (Québec)): Listening and information line 1-877-276-7772, in French and English.

We list organizations whose contact details we could check on their own websites. Inclusion is not an endorsement, and Tinnitus Evidence has no connection with any of them.

Professional support for tinnitus distress

Talking therapies, especially cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), have the strongest evidence for reducing how much tinnitus affects daily life. They don't make the sound quieter, but they help many people sleep, cope and feel better. Ask your doctor or audiologist what is available where you live. If sleep is the main problem, ask about CBT for insomnia.
