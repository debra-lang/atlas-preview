Tinnitus reduction in single-sided deafness: cochlear implant, bone conduction device and CROS hearing aid (CINGLE randomized controlled trial)

Record details

  • Authors: Wendrich AW, Assouly KKS, van Heteren JAA, Peters JPM, Grolman W, Stokroos RJ, Versnel H, Smit AL
  • Journal / source: Frontiers in Neurology (2024)
  • Design: Single-centre RCT: 2:3:3 allocation to cochlear implant, or to trial periods of a bone conduction device then CROS (or the reverse); after the trial periods patients chose a device or no treatment, so the non-implant comparison groups are self-selected
  • Sample: 120 adults with single-sided deafness randomized; at the start of follow-up: 28 implanted, 25 bone conduction device, 34 CROS, 26 no treatment
  • Primary endpoint: Tinnitus outcomes (THI, TQ, VAS, HADS) were secondary; the trial was designed around hearing in single-sided deafness
  • Randomized: Yes · Sham/placebo-controlled: No · Independent of the manufacturer: No

What this research found

At 24 months the implant group improved by a median of 23 THI points, 17 TQ points and 60 VAS points from baseline (all p<0.001), already at 3 months; the bone conduction group improved on TQ and VAS; CROS and no treatment showed no significant change. Implant vs no treatment was significant on THI, TQ and VAS; implant vs the device groups was significant for VAS but not reported significant for THI or TQ.

Tinnitus was a secondary outcome in a single-centre trial; the significance threshold was Bonferroni-corrected, and missing data rose to about 20% by 24 months. This is a trial in people with single-sided deafness, not evidence for implantation in people without qualifying hearing loss.

Limitations and conflicts of interest

Secondary outcome; single centre; self-selected non-implant comparison groups; manufacturer funding; single-sided deafness only.

Funding / conflicts: Funded by Cochlear Ltd, a cochlear implant manufacturer (the funder is reported to have had no role in design, analysis or publication); one author was employed by Cochlear Technology Centre Belgium; the trial used Cochlear implants and Baha devices.
