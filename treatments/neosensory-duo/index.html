Neosensory Duo (wristband bimodal) for Tinnitus: What Does the Evidence Show?

Neuromodulation

Current evidence

A consumer wristband borrowing the bimodal idea — sold on one small company-run study.

Evidence strength: Weak (1/5) · Tier 3 — experimental/emerging · Evidence independence: Primarily sponsor-supported.

A vibrating wristband paired with tone playback from an app (10 minutes/day), sold directly to consumers as a wellness device.

Did tinnitus loudness improve?

None shown. No loudness data.

Did tinnitus distress improve?

Limited. One company-run study: TFI −17.9 in the experimental group — unblinded, no sham vibration, all authors are company employees.

Loudness means the tinnitus percept itself became quieter (psychoacoustic matching or loudness ratings). Distress means questionnaire scores such as THI/TFI, sleep, anxiety or quality of life improved — the sound may be unchanged. A THI/TFI improvement is never evidence the tinnitus got quieter.

How strong is the evidence?

Marketing currently exceeds evidence: 1/5 until independent controlled data exist.

Has the result been independently replicated?

None yet. Single company-run trial; no replication.

What are the limitations?

  • Single small manufacturer-run study in a minor journal
  • No sham control — participants knew their group
  • Wrist stimulation lacks the anatomical rationale of tongue/neck approaches

Conflicts of interest: All study authors are Neosensory employees or its founder/CEO.

What should patients know about safety?

Safety evidence: Strong. Benign (vibration + sound).

Is it available?

Regulatory status: Consumer wellness device (no FDA authorization found). Sold as general wellness; no FDA clearance/authorization for tinnitus treatment located.

Availability: Direct-to-consumer online · Europe: Ships internationally · Cost (approx.): ≈$500 device + subscription (varies)

What the studies found

Bimodal stimulation using vibration on the skin (Neosensory Duo)

Perrotta MV, Kohler I, Eagleman DM. · International Tinnitus Journal · 2023 · N=45 (22 experimental, 23 control analyzed) · Controlled study, tones + wrist vibration vs tones-only

TFI change −17.9 (SD 18.2) with the wristband vs −7.5 (SD 15.4) with tones only (between-group p=.04). No sham vibration — participants knew whether they wore the wristband.

The larger −21.8 figure the company quotes applies only to the baseline-TFI≥50 subgroup (n=18). Numeric results sourced from the full-text PDF (International Tinnitus Journal); not present in the indexed abstract.

PubMed 38050877

Related treatments

  • Auricle bisensory stimulation (Susan Shore device) — The only treatment with rigorous randomized evidence of making tinnitus quieter — still stuck in the lab.
  • Lenire (bimodal tongue stimulation) — Pairs sound with mild tongue stimulation — the first and only device of its type the FDA has authorized for…
  • Paired vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) — A promising pilot, then silence — the sponsor took the technology to stroke rehab instead.
  • Acoustic coordinated reset (Desyncra) — A elegant theory whose confirmatory trial came back empty.
  • Transcutaneous auricular VNS (taVNS) — Non-surgical vagus nerve stimulation via an ear clip — small pilots so far, with the largest trial ever now…
