Sleep and difficult nights

Tinnitus often seems louder at night and in the early morning,1 and around half of people with tinnitus say they sleep poorly.2 If you're reading this awake, you're not alone, and there are things that can help.

Having a difficult night? Try this now

Tonight

Right now

Immediate steps to make tonight easier

  • Put on a soft, steady sound you like, such as a fan, rain or low music. Keep it a little quieter than your tinnitus: it doesn't have to cover it.4
  • Let yourself rest. You don't have to force sleep; resting in bed is still helpful.3
  • When you've finished reading, put your phone face down, or set a timer on your bedtime sound.

If you feel desperate tonight, you don't have to cope alone. Talk to someone now

Later tonight

Still awake?

What to do when sleep won't come

  • If you've been wide awake for what feels like 20 to 30 minutes (guess, don't check the clock), get up and do something calm in dim light. Go back to bed when you feel sleepy.3, 6 Take care on the stairs in the dark.Awake 20–30 minCalm, dim lightBack when sleepy

If your mind is racing

  • Try a worry list earlier in the evening. Spend 15 to 20 minutes writing down what's on your mind and what you could do about it. Worries are then less likely to arrive at bedtime.3
  • Give yourself an hour to wind down before bed, away from work and bright screens.3, 14
  • Keep the bed for sleep. If you can't sleep, get up for a while rather than lying there getting frustrated.6
  • Relaxation can help, such as slow breathing or relaxing your muscles one group at a time.6

Tomorrow

After a bad night: steps that may help the following night

  • MorningGet up at your usual time, even after a poor night. Sleeping in can make the next night harder.6, 14
  • During the dayAvoid long naps. If you must nap, keep it short and early in the day.3, 6
  • During the dayStay active in the day, but not with hard exercise close to bedtime.3
  • EveningGo easy on caffeine and alcohol. Avoid tea, coffee and alcohol for about 6 hours before bed. Alcohol and over-the-counter sleep aids can make you wake earlier.3, 14
  • If you feel very sleepyBe careful driving if you feel very sleepy.

One bad night is hard, but it is usually nothing to worry about. Take extra care tomorrow if you need to drive and feel very drowsy.

Using sound at night

A quiet bedroom makes tinnitus stand out. Gentle background sound gives your ears something else to hear.3

  • Choose something pleasant that doesn't demand attention: a fan, rain, waves, soft music or a quiet radio. If a sound annoys you, try another.4
  • Keep the volume low, a little quieter than your tinnitus, so you can still just hear it. Very loud sound can give relief in the moment, but the tinnitus may seem louder when it stops.4
  • Timer or all night? Either is fine. A timer suits you if you mainly struggle to fall asleep. Leaving a soft sound on helps if you wake in the early hours.4
  • Use a bedside speaker or a pillow speaker rather than earbuds all night, and keep it at a gentle level. A pillow speaker is quieter for a partner.4

On an iPhone: Settings, then Accessibility, Audio & Visual, Background Sounds. Choose rain, ocean or another sound, set the volume, and use the timer if you like. If you want the sound to keep playing with the screen locked, turn off Stop Sounds When Locked.5

Sound can make nights more comfortable. It hasn't been shown to reduce tinnitus over time, and expensive devices aren't proven to do better than a fan or a free app.11, 12

If poor sleep has gone on for a while: CBT for insomnia

CBT for insomnia (CBT-I) is the recommended first treatment for long-term insomnia. It suits people with other health conditions too, and it can be done online.6, 7, 8 It is a short course that changes the habits and worries that keep poor sleep going.

In people with tinnitus, the early evidence is encouraging. In one trial, more than 8 in 10 people who had CBT-I said their sleep clearly improved. That compared with 47% in an audiology-based sleep programme and 20% in a support group. Their tinnitus distress fell too.9 A review of four smaller trials found sleep improved on average.10

What it's like

It usually takes several weeks. You may feel more tired at first, especially if it includes "sleep restriction": spending less time in bed for a while, so that sleep becomes deeper. If you drive for work, or have bipolar disorder or seizures, do that part with a clinician.6 CBT-I: our evidence page

Where to find it

  • UK: ask your GP. NICE recommends the digital programme Sleepio; in England whether it's offered depends on your local NHS area, and it's free for adults in Scotland.19, 20
  • US: the VA's free Insomnia Coach app is a self-help programme based on CBT-I, available to everyone.21 (The VA's CBT-i Coach app is meant to be used with a therapist.) SleepioRx is an FDA-cleared digital CBT-I programme; ask your doctor.22 Sleep specialists who offer CBT-I are listed by the Society of Behavioral Sleep Medicine.
  • Australia: the free online THIS WAY UP Insomnia Program, which you can do on your own or with a clinician.23
  • Elsewhere: ask your doctor about CBT-I, including online CBT-I.

See a doctor before starting a self-help programme if you snore loudly, stop breathing in your sleep or feel very sleepy in the day. The same goes if you have bipolar disorder or seizures, or are at risk of falls.19, 21

Sleeping tablets and melatonin

  • Sleeping tablets are only for short-term use, usually a few days or weeks. They can have serious side effects and you can become dependent on them.8, 14 If you already take them, don't stop suddenly: talk to your prescriber first.
  • Melatonin doesn't treat tinnitus.16 For sleep, US sleep specialists advise against it for long-term insomnia.15 In the UK it's prescription-only, and mainly for short-term use in people aged 55 and over.17 Supplements sold in the US are often wrongly labelled.18 Ask a doctor before trying it. Supplements: the evidence
  • Other medicines, such as antidepressants, aren't a routine treatment for tinnitus itself. A doctor may still use them for sleep or mood problems.13, 16

When to talk to a doctor, and what to say

If poor sleep happens on 3 or more nights a week for 3 months or more and affects your days, doctors call it long-term insomnia.14, 15 You don't have to wait that long: see a doctor sooner if it's making it hard to cope.14

You could say: "Tinnitus is stopping me sleeping on [number] nights a week, for [weeks or months]. It's affecting my [work / mood / concentration]. Could you check my sleep, for example with the Insomnia Severity Index, and refer me for CBT-I?"12

Also mention loud snoring, gasping in your sleep or feeling very sleepy in the day. These can be signs of sleep apnoea, when breathing stops and starts during sleep, which is treatable.19

Keep a simple sleep diary for a week or two: when you went to bed, roughly when you fell asleep, when you woke and when you got up. It helps your doctor see the pattern.8 Prepare for your appointment

What the research says

  • Strong guideline support CBT for insomnia is the recommended first treatment for long-term insomnia in adults, including online CBT-I.6, 7, 8
  • Encouraging, early In people with tinnitus, CBT-I improved sleep in one 102-person trial and, on average, in a review of four small trials.9, 10
  • Guideline advice Getting up when you can't sleep, keeping a regular wake-up time, and relaxation are recommended parts of insomnia treatment. Sleep tips on their own aren't recommended as the main treatment.6
  • Comfort, not cure Gentle sound is an option in the US tinnitus guideline. Studies haven't shown that sound devices reduce tinnitus over time, and none measured sleep.11, 13
  • Practical tip Sound volume, worry lists and phone habits are expert advice from tinnitus charities, with little direct research.3, 4
